Week 1

Meeting language

The verbs people use when a meeting is actually deciding something.

01 Today

Align before you leave

align

Get to the same picture of the goal or the decision. A nod is not alignment.

“Before we leave, are we aligned that phase one is the portal only, not the mobile app?”

alignment

The shared picture itself. You either have it with someone, or you don’t.

“We don’t have alignment with Finance yet, so I won’t quote a date to the client.”

are we aligned?

A closing check, not a request for applause. Ask it while people can still disagree.

“Are we aligned on the owner, or only on the idea?”

Task Say the closing check out loud, then write one alignment question from a project you are actually in.

02 Today

Push back with a reason

push back

Resist a plan, a date, or a scope change, and give the reason in the same breath.

“I’d push back on a Friday launch. Legal still hasn’t cleared the consent copy.”

pushback

The noun. Name it when you expect resistance, so it doesn’t arrive as a surprise.

“Expect pushback from Sales if we cut the discount. I’ll take that conversation, not you.”

hold the line

Keep a position you already own. Don’t soften it just to end the silence.

“We can discuss packaging, but I’m going to hold the line on the price.”

Task Speak one push-back about a real deadline: the no, then the reason, in a single sentence.

03 Today

Flag it, then escalate

flag

Raise a gap or a risk early, while the room can still do something about it.

“I want to flag that the client hasn’t signed the SOW, so we shouldn’t staff this yet.”

escalate

Take it to someone with authority to unblock it. Stronger than a flag, and later.

“If procurement hasn’t moved by Thursday, we escalate to the sponsor. Not before.”

call out

Name the problem directly. Useful, and sharper — it can sound like blame if you aim it at a person.

“I’ll call out the missing data in the note. I won’t call out the analyst in the meeting.”

Task Write two lines: what you would flag today, and the condition that would make you escalate it.

04 Today

Own the outcome

own

Drive an outcome to the end. In loose speech it covers both the work and the answerability.

“I’ll own the client update. I need the figures from you by 3, and I’ll write the note.”

accountable

The person who answers for the result. On a RACI chart there is one; others may still do the work.

“Priya stays accountable for the forecast. The rest of us are feeding it, not owning the number.”

I’ve got it

A short claim of ownership. Only say it if you can actually carry the next step.

“I’ve got the follow-up note. If you also send one, the client will get two versions.”

Task Write one sentence that names what you own and one thing you deliberately do not.

05 Today

Park it, or table it

park

Set a topic aside and come back later. The safe verb in a mixed US and UK room.

“Let’s park pricing until Finance is actually in the room, and pick it up at 3.”

table (US)

Postpone the discussion. In American business English you are taking it off the table.

“Let’s table the hiring plan until next week, once we have the headcount number.”

table (UK)

Present something for discussion now. In British English the verb means the opposite.

“I’d like to table the hiring plan while the budget owners are here, not after they leave.”

US / UK. The noun is shared: “on the table” means up for discussion in both. The verb is the trap. If the room is mixed, say park and name the moment you will come back.

Task Say the US sentence and the UK sentence out loud, then write a park sentence that names when you will return.

06 Today

Bandwidth and scope

bandwidth

Time and attention you actually have. Capacity says the same thing with less jargon.

“I don’t have the bandwidth for a second design review this week. I can do Monday.”

scope

The boundary of what is included. If it isn’t named, people will assume their version.

“Scope for this phase is onboarding and billing. Reporting waits until the next release.”

scope creep

Work added after the boundary was set, often as a small favor. Name it or it becomes the project.

“A new dashboard wasn’t in the contract. That’s scope creep, not a quick add.”

Task Rewrite the bandwidth sentence for your actual week, and name one thing that is out of scope.

07 Today

Circle back, precisely

circle back

Return to a point later. Without a person and a day, it is a way of ending the topic, not moving it.

“I’ll circle back Thursday with Ana’s numbers — a recommendation, not a recap.”

take offline

Move a side conversation out of this meeting so the group can continue. It does not mean drop it.

“You and I should take the contract wording offline. This room doesn’t need the redline.”

stakeholder

Someone who can move the decision, or who has to live with it. Not a synonym for everyone invited.

“The stakeholder is the plant manager. Copying the whole site list doesn’t make them one.”

Task Replace a vague “I’ll circle back” with your own sentence: who, what, and which day.

Week 2

Nuance pairs

Close words. The gap is small, and it changes what you are promising.

08 Today

Agree is not align

agree

You accept a specific claim or a specific proposal. It is about that point, not the whole direction.

“I agree the margin is thin. That’s a fact, not a plan.”

align

You share the direction. You can agree the problem is real and still not be aligned on the response.

“I’m not aligned on discounting our way to the number. I’d rather cut scope.”

agree, but not aligned

The useful split in the room: yes to the diagnosis, no to the remedy.

“I agree we need a decision today. I’m not aligned that the decision is to sign.”

Task Write one sentence where you agree on the goal and refuse the method.

09 Today

Suggest, recommend, propose

suggest

Offer an idea with low commitment. Easy for the other person to decline without a fight.

“I’d suggest we sleep on the vendor. I’m not ready to die on that hill tonight.”

recommend

Advise from judgment or evidence, and be willing to stand behind it.

“I recommend vendor B. The support terms are the difference, not the demo.”

propose

Put a concrete motion in front of the room: this action, this owner, this time.

“I propose we decide in tomorrow’s standup, not over chat, and that Sam records the call.”

Task Speak three short lines about the same decision: a suggestion, a recommendation, and a proposal.

10 Today

Delay, defer, postpone

delay

Make something later than it should have been, or admit that it has already slipped.

“The audit delayed the dates. I’m not going to pretend the plan absorbed it.”

postpone

Move a planned event to a later slot. The event is still happening; the calendar changes.

“Let’s postpone the town hall to Tuesday. Same agenda, different room.”

defer

Put a decision off on purpose. Also defer to: you accept someone else’s judgment over yours.

“I’ll defer the vendor choice to you. I won’t defer the security review — that stays this week.”

Task Use all three verbs in one spoken sentence about a meeting you know.

11 Today

Concern, risk, issue

concern

A worry you want named before it becomes damage. It is not yet a broken thing.

“My concern is the tone of the mail. It reads as a promise we may not keep.”

risk

A future event that might hurt the work. It has not happened; it has a chance and an impact.

“The risk is the buyer reads “target” as a commitment and plans a campaign around it.”

issue

A problem that exists now and needs an owner. Don’t use it for a mild preference.

“The issue is we already promised the 12th in writing. The concern is how we unwind that.”

Task Take one real worry and say whether it is a concern, a risk, or an issue, and why.

12 Today

Update, status, progress

update

What changed since the last conversation. If nothing changed, say that — it is still an update.

“Update: Legal returned the draft this morning. Nothing else moved.”

status

Where the work stands right now. A snapshot, not a story about how hard everyone worked.

“Status is amber. We are not blocked on people; we are blocked on the indemnity clause.”

progress

Movement toward the result. Activity, meetings, and drafts are not progress by themselves.

“We’ve had a lot of activity and no progress on the clause that actually gates the signature.”

Task Give a twenty-second spoken update that separates status from real progress.

13 Today

Feedback, input, sign-off

feedback

A reaction meant to change the work. Ask for it on a draft, not on a decision you have already made.

“I need feedback on the tone, not a rewrite, by Wednesday close of play.”

input

Facts or views you want before you decide. You are still the one who will decide.

“I want input from Support today on whether this SLA is even operable.”

sign-off

Explicit approval to proceed. “Looks fine” is not sign-off unless you have agreed that those words count.

“I need your sign-off as a yes in this thread before anything goes to the client.”

Task Write one sentence that asks for only one of the three, and name the person.

14 Today

Urgent is not the priority

urgent

Time is short. Someone’s clock is running. That alone does not make it the most important work.

“Their board mail is urgent. It has to go tonight, whatever else is true.”

important

The outcome cares. A thing can be important for months and still not be today’s work.

“The rebrand is important and not urgent. It can wait until the outage is stable.”

priority

What you will actually do first when both lists are long. Say it out loud or the loudest voice wins.

“The outage is the priority. I’ll answer the board mail after the customer update is out.”

Task Name one thing in your week that is urgent, one that is important, and which one is the priority.

Week 3

Email and written tone

How a message sounds when you cannot use your voice to soften it.

15 Today

Hedge once

hedge

Limit how hard you claim something, without sounding unsure of your own name. One hedge is enough.

“I’d be careful about calling this a commitment. We haven’t priced the second year.”

My read is

Signals interpretation, not a verified fact. Senior, and still open to correction.

“My read is they will not approve a multi-year deal this quarter.”

It looks as though

A precise softener when the evidence is incomplete. Don’t stack it with maybe, just, and I think.

“It looks as though the client is cooling on the upsell. I don’t have that in writing yet.”

Task Rewrite “This plan will fail” with a single precise hedge, then say it as you would in a steering meeting.

16 Today

Firm, still polite

I’m not able to

A boundary. You are refusing the request, not confessing a personal weakness.

“I’m not able to approve this version. The liability cap has been removed.”

We’ll need

States the condition for a yes. No apology attached, and no false door.

“We’ll need the revised numbers before I can sign. I can turn them around the same day.”

That doesn’t work for us

Clear, and not an insult. Use it when a softer no would be read as a negotiation.

“A net-90 term doesn’t work for us. We can talk about net-45 with a deposit.”

Task Write a two-sentence no that does not apologize for the no.

17 Today

Ask so they can answer

Could you

A real request in polite form, not a hypothetical. The next words should be the action.

“Could you send the signed PDF, not a photo of the signature page?”

Please

Direct, and right when you already have the standing to ask. Don’t wrap it in three sorrys.

“Please send the redline by end of day. I’ll take comments in the document, not in chat.”

by when

The clock, plus why that clock exists. A deadline without a reason feels like pressure.

“I need a yes or no by 4 pm Wednesday so I can brief the CFO at 9.”

Task Take a vague “let me know your thoughts” and rewrite it with an owner, an action, and a deadline.

18 Today

Bad news, first line

BLUF

Bottom line up front. The news is sentence one. Warmth after, not before.

“We will not hit the 3 November release.”

one-line cause

Why, in a single line. A long prelude reads as if you are hiding the point.

“The payments integration failed certification on Tuesday.”

the next step

What you propose to do, or what you need from them. Bad news without a move is just a sigh.

“I propose we move the release to 17 November, and I can tell the client today if you agree.”

Task Write three short lines about a real or realistic slip: the news, the cause, the next step. Say them in that order.

19 Today

Follow up with the ask

follow up

A second touch that restates the ask. “Just checking in” with no ask is noise.

“Following up on Monday’s note — I still need a yes or no on the travel budget.”

bump

Casual and internal. Fine in a team chat. Thin with a client or a senior you don’t know well.

“Bumping this for the thread: are we approved to book, or should I hold?”

easy to reply

Make the answer a choice, not an essay. You get decisions from busy people that way.

“Reply with “book” or “hold” by tomorrow noon and I’ll take it from there.”

Task Write a follow-up you could send without rereading the whole thread.

20 Today

Who this is for

loop in

Add a person who now has a reason to be on the thread, and write that reason in the same line.

“Looping in Helena, who owns renewals and needs to confirm the price.”

FYI

For their information. Say it when you truly expect no reply, or everyone assumes they must act.

“FYI for the wider group — no action unless the date is wrong.”

the audience

Don’t add a crowd to create pressure. Every name should have a job: decide, act, or merely know.

“I’m keeping this to the three of us until there’s a decision worth sharing.”

Task Write the one-line intro you would use to add someone to a thread, and state whether they must act.

21 Today

The subject does the work

subject line

Carry the decision or the deadline. “Quick question” and “Hello” throw that work away.

“Decision by Friday: keep or cut the pilot.”

one ask

A single action when the stakes are a decision. Context belongs under the ask, not before it.

“One ask: reply keep or cut by Friday at 11. Usage numbers are below if you want them.”

FYI in the subject

If there is nothing to do, say so where they will see it before they open the mail.

“FYI only: pilot notes from Thursday, no reply needed.”

Task Write a subject line and a first sentence for a decision you are actually waiting on.

Week 4

Fluency under pressure

Nine days. What to say when the room is waiting and the sentence has to land.

22 Today

Disagree in one breath

I see it differently

Disagree with the idea, and still sound like a partner. Don’t sandwich it between empty compliments.

“I see it differently. A softer deadline saves the team and misses the buyer’s window.”

I’m not with you on that

Firmer than “I see it differently,” still aimed at the point and not at the person.

“I’m not with you on cutting QA. The date matters less than a failed first week.”

I’d rather

The alternative, in the same breath. An objection with no other path is just a brake.

“I’d rather keep the date and cut the reporting pack than ship a quieter failure.”

Task Speak a disagreement in three beats: the point, the reason, the alternative.

23 Today

Buy time, name the clock

I don’t want to guess

Buys a moment without pretending you know. Better than a confident wrong number.

“I don’t want to guess at churn in this room.”

Let me check before I commit

Separates a fast answer from a promise. Use it when they are about to write your words down.

“Let me check the dashboard before I commit to a figure you’ll repeat to the board.”

I’ll come back at

Time plus a product: a number, a name, a yes or a no. “I’ll get back to you” has neither.

“I’ll come back at 3 with the figure from the dashboard, not a range.”

Task Replace “I’ll get back to you” with a clock and a specific thing you will bring.

24 Today

Play the decisions back

Let me play that back

Restate the decisions, not the whole discussion. You are checking the record, not performing a summary.

“Let me play that back: phase one ships on the 12th, and design owns the empty states.”

What I heard

Frames the recap as your understanding, so people can correct it before the note goes out.

“What I heard is that pricing stays open. Correct me if that was actually decided.”

What did I miss?

Invites the missing decision. Silence after a playback is how wrong notes get sent.

“What did I miss that we actually decided, as opposed to discussed?”

Task Summarize an imaginary half-hour meeting in under thirty seconds, out loud: decisions only.

25 Today

Close the meeting

close

End on decisions, owners, and dates. A good discussion is not a result.

“To close: the launch stays on the 12th. That is the decision.”

owner and date

Every decision leaves with a name and a clock. Unowned tasks die in the notes.

“Mina sends the note by 5 today. If your name isn’t on an action, you don’t have one.”

not decided

Say what was parked, and who has it, or the room will remember three different versions.

“The pricing exception is not decided. It’s parked with me for Monday.”

Task Close a fake meeting in four sentences: decision, owner, date, and one thing explicitly still open.

26 Today

Small talk with a purpose

a real detail

Open on something specific you actually know. Weather is a blank, unless the weather is the business.

“You mentioned the Osaka visit — was that the supplier, or the new warehouse?”

I ask because

Tie the minute of chat to the meeting, so it doesn’t feel like an interview.

“I ask because it changes the capacity number we’re about to freeze.”

I’ll leave it there

End it. Small talk that cannot stop costs you more trust than skipping it.

“I’ll leave it there. Shall we start with the forecast?”

Task Prepare one opener you could use with a stakeholder you actually meet, and say it out loud once.

27 Today

Come in, and finish

Can I come in on that?

A short interrupt that names the point. “Sorry, sorry, sorry” without a point is just noise.

“Can I come in on the date? If we leave it vague, this meeting hasn’t decided anything.”

jump in

The usual American verb for the same move. Slightly more abrupt; fine when the pace is fast.

“Sorry to jump in — the only decision this hour can make is the date.”

I wasn’t finished

A calm reclaim when someone cuts you off. Apologize for the overlap if you must, not for the content.

“I wasn’t finished on the risk. Twenty seconds, then it’s yours.”

Task Practice one interrupt and one reclaim out loud. Keep each under twenty seconds.

28 Today

What you know, and what you don’t

What I know is

The part you can stand behind. Start here when you are put on the spot.

“What I know is the pilot closed at 18 percent.”

What I’m inferring is

The step beyond the evidence. Label it, and you sound careful rather than lost.

“What I’m inferring is that a full rollout looks similar. That is a guess, not a result.”

I wouldn’t commit to that yet

Stops a hypothesis becoming a target while you are still talking.

“I wouldn’t commit the quarter to that yet. Give me a week with the enterprise cohort.”

Task Answer a hard question in two spoken parts: what is known, and what is not yet known.

29 Today

Pin down the vague word

When you say…

Repeat their vague word. Soon, ASAP, some, support, later — don’t translate it in your head.

“When you say soon, I want to check which soon you mean.”

Do you mean A or B?

Offer two meanings that would change the work. An open “can you clarify?” is easier to wave off.

“Do you mean a helpdesk from day one, or only help with the install?”

It changes

Say why you are asking, in one clause, so the question doesn’t sound like you weren’t listening.

“It changes the estimate. Those are different teams.”

Task Pick a vague word you heard recently and turn it into one sharp question with two meanings.

30 Today

Commit, or don’t

I can commit

A result and a time you control. If both aren’t yours, you don’t have a commitment to give.

“I can commit to a first draft on Thursday at noon.”

I can’t commit

A clean limit. More useful than a soft yes you will miss.

“I can’t commit to the client’s approval this week. That isn’t mine to give.”

I’ll try

Polite, and not a yes. Don’t offer it when someone is staffing or scheduling against your word.

“I won’t say I’ll try. I’ll either send the draft Thursday, or I’ll tell you Wednesday that I can’t.”

Task Say one real commitment for this week and one thing you explicitly will not commit to. Then mark the day done.
